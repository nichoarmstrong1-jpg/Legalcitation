## T.1.1 Federal Judicial and Legislative Materials

[
Overview of T1.1
This table, found on pages 257 through 260 of the Bluebook, tells you exactly how to cite federal judicial opinions and federal legislative materials. It covers every level of the federal court system and the major statutory sources. The table directs you to visit uscourts.gov for more details on the federal court structure, including which district courts exist and what geographic territory each court of appeals covers.
Supreme Court of the United States (abbreviated U.S.)
When you are citing a Supreme Court case, the table tells you that your first choice should always be to cite to the United States Reports, abbreviated "U.S." If the case is not yet published there, you should cite to the Supreme Court Reporter (abbreviated "S. Ct.") as your second choice, then the Lawyers' Edition (abbreviated "L. Ed." or "L. Ed. 2d") as your third choice, and finally United States Law Week (abbreviated "U.S.L.W.") as your last resort.
The United States Reports has been the official reporter since 1790, but the early volumes were published under the names of individual reporters. From volume 91 onward (1875 to the present), you simply cite to "U.S." with no special notation. For the earlier nominative reporter volumes, the table explains that the reporter named Wallace covered 1863 to 1874 and you would cite, for example, as "68 U.S. (1 Wall.)." The reporter named Black covered 1861 to 1862, and you would cite as "66 U.S. (1 Black)." Howard covered 1843 to 1860, cited as "42 U.S. (1 How.)." Peters covered 1828 to 1842, cited as "26 U.S. (1 Pet.)." Wheaton covered 1816 to 1827, cited as "14 U.S. (1 Wheat.)." Cranch covered 1801 to 1815, cited as "5 U.S. (1 Cranch)." And Dallas covered 1790 to 1800, cited as "1 U.S. (1 Dall.)." The Supreme Court Reporter has been publishing since 1882, the Lawyers' Edition since 1790, and United States Law Week since 1933.
Circuit Justices (for example, Sotomayor, J., in chambers)
This section explains that when a single Supreme Court Justice acts in their capacity as a Circuit Justice — meaning they are ruling on an emergency matter assigned to their circuit — the same order of citation preference applies: cite to U.S. first, then S. Ct., then a reporter called Rapps' In Chambers Opinions (abbreviated "Rapp," covering 1925 to present), then L. Ed. or L. Ed. 2d, and finally U.S.L.W. The table also notes that some cases handled by Circuit Justices ended up published in other reporters entirely, and in those situations you should cite to whatever reporter the case appears in and note the Circuit Justice parenthetically.
Courts of Appeals (for example, 2d Cir. or D.C. Cir.)
These are the intermediate federal appellate courts. The table says they were previously called Circuit Courts of Appeals. The instruction is to cite to the Federal Reporter, which has gone through several series: F., F.2d, F.3d, and F.4th, covering from 1891 to the present. There is also the Federal Appendix, abbreviated "F. App'x," which has published cases from 2001 to the present. The Federal Appendix contains unpublished or non-precedential opinions.
Circuit Courts (for example, C.C.S.D.N.Y. or C.C.D. Cal.)
These were the old trial-level federal courts that were abolished in 1912. For cases decided in these courts, the table says to cite to the Federal Reporter (abbreviated "F.") for cases from 1880 to 1912, and to Federal Cases (abbreviated "F. Cas.") for cases from 1789 to 1880. When you cite to Federal Cases, you must include the case number in parentheses at the end of the citation.
Temporary Emergency Court of Appeals, Emergency Court of Appeals, and Commerce Court
The table groups these three specialized courts together. The Temporary Emergency Court of Appeals existed from 1971 to 1993. The Emergency Court of Appeals existed from 1942 to 1961. The Commerce Court existed from 1910 to 1913. For all three, you cite to the Federal Reporter — either F. or F.2d — covering the years 1910 through 1993.
United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (abbreviated Fed. Cir.)
This court was created in 1982 and is the successor to two older courts: the Court of Customs and Patent Appeals (abbreviated C.C.P.A., which was originally called the Court of Customs Appeals, abbreviated Ct. Cust. App.) and the appellate side of the Court of Claims (abbreviated Ct. Cl.). The table says to cite to the Federal Reporter (F., F.2d, F.3d, or F.4th) if the case appears there, which covers 1910 to the present. If the case is not in the Federal Reporter, you should cite to the court's own official reporter: Court of Claims Reports (abbreviated "Ct. Cl.") covers 1956 to 1982, Court of Customs and Patent Appeals Reports (abbreviated "C.C.P.A.") covers 1929 to 1982, and Court of Customs Appeals Reports (abbreviated "Ct. Cust.") covers 1910 to 1929.
United States Court of Federal Claims (abbreviated Fed. Cl.)
This court was created in 1992. Before that, it was called the United States Claims Court (abbreviated Cl. Ct.), created in 1982, and before that it was the trial-level side of the old Court of Claims. The table says to cite to one of these reporters: the Federal Claims Reporter (abbreviated "Fed. Cl.") from 1992 to the present, the United States Claims Court Reporter (abbreviated "Cl. Ct.") from 1983 to 1992, the Federal Reporter second series (F.2d) for the periods 1930 to 1932 and 1960 to 1982, the Federal Supplement (abbreviated "F. Supp.") from 1932 to 1960, or the Court of Claims Reports (abbreviated "Ct. Cl.") from 1863 to 1982.
United States Court of International Trade (abbreviated Ct. Int'l Trade)
This court was created in 1980 and was formerly known as the United States Customs Court (abbreviated Cust. Ct.), which was created in 1926. The table instructs you to cite to the official reporters if possible. The Court of International Trade Reports (abbreviated "Ct. Int'l Trade") covers 1980 to the present, and Customs Court Reports (abbreviated "Cust. Ct.") covers 1938 to 1980. If those are unavailable, your fallback options in order of preference are: the Federal Supplement (F. Supp., F. Supp. 2d, or F. Supp. 3d) from 1980 onward, the Customs Bulletin and Decisions (abbreviated "Cust. B. & Dec.") from 1967 onward, and the International Trade Reporter Decisions (abbreviated "I.T.R.D. (BL)") from 1980 onward.
District Courts (for example, D. Mass. or S.D.N.Y.)
These are the federal trial courts. For cases decided after 1932, the table says to cite to the Federal Supplement (F. Supp., F. Supp. 2d, or F. Supp. 3d), Federal Rules Decisions (abbreviated "F.R.D." from 1938 onward), or West's Bankruptcy Reporter (abbreviated "B.R." from 1979 onward) if the case appears in any of those. If not, cite to Federal Rules Service (abbreviated "Fed. R. Serv.," with second and third series published by Callaghan and West respectively) from 1938 onward. For cases before 1932, cite to the Federal Reporter (F. or F.2d) for 1880 to 1932, or to Federal Cases (F. Cas.) for 1789 to 1880. Again, when citing Federal Cases, include the case number parenthetically.
Bankruptcy Courts and Bankruptcy Appellate Panels (for example, Bankr. N.D. Cal. or B.A.P. 1st Cir.)
The table says to cite to the Bankruptcy Reporter (abbreviated "B.R.") from 1979 to the present. If the case is not in the Bankruptcy Reporter, cite to a service as described in Bluebook Rule 19.
Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation (abbreviated J.P.M.L.) and Special Court under the Regional Rail Reorganization Act (abbreviated Reg'l Rail Reorg. Ct.)
The Judicial Panel was created in 1968, and the Special Court existed from 1973 to 1997. For both, the table says to cite to the Federal Supplement (F. Supp., F. Supp. 2d, or F. Supp. 3d) from 1968 onward.
Tax Court (abbreviated T.C.) and Board of Tax Appeals (abbreviated B.T.A.)
The Tax Court was created in 1942 and was previously called the Board of Tax Appeals. The table says to cite to the United States Tax Court Reports (abbreviated "T.C.") from 1942 to the present, or to the Reports of the United States Board of Tax Appeals (abbreviated "B.T.A.") for 1924 to 1942. If the case is not in those official reporters, cite to Tax Court Memorandum Decisions: the CCH version (abbreviated "T.C.M. (CCH)") covers 1942 to the present, the Prentice-Hall version (abbreviated "T.C.M. (P-H)") covers 1942 to 1991, and the RIA version (abbreviated "T.C.M. (RIA)") covers 1991 to the present. For the earlier Board of Tax Appeals, memorandum decisions are cited as "B.T.A.M. (P-H)" covering 1928 to 1942.
United States Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (abbreviated Vet. App.)
This court was created in 1988 and was previously called the United States Court of Veterans Appeals. The table says to cite to West's Veterans Appeals Reporter (abbreviated "Vet. App.") from 1990 to the present.
United States Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces (abbreviated C.A.A.F.) and its predecessor, the Court of Military Appeals (abbreviated C.M.A.)
The table says to cite to the Decisions of the United States Court of Military Appeals (abbreviated "C.M.A.") for 1951 to 1975 if the case is there. Other reporters include West's Military Justice Reporter (abbreviated "M.J.") from 1978 to the present and Court Martial Reports (abbreviated "C.M.R.") from 1951 to 1975.
Military Service Courts of Criminal Appeals (for example, A. Ct. Crim. App., A.F. Ct. Crim. App., C.G. Ct. Crim. App., N-M. Ct. Crim. App.)
These were previously called Courts of Military Review and before that, Boards of Review. For cases after 1950, the table says to cite to West's Military Justice Reporter (abbreviated "M.J.") from 1975 onward, or Court Martial Reports (abbreviated "C.M.R.") from 1951 to 1975. For cases before 1951, cite to the official reporter of the relevant military service branch.
Statutory Compilations
The table says your first choice for citing federal statutes is always the United States Code, abbreviated "U.S.C." The citation format is the title number, then "U.S.C.," then the section symbol and section number, with the year optionally in parentheses. Title 26 of the U.S.C. (the tax code) may be abbreviated as "I.R.C." (Internal Revenue Code). If the statute is not available in the U.S.C., you have several alternatives: the United States Code Annotated (abbreviated "U.S.C.A.," published by West), the United States Code Service (abbreviated "U.S.C.S.," published by LexisNexis), Gould's United States Code Unannotated (abbreviated "U.S.C.U.," published by Gould), and the Revised Statutes of the United States (abbreviated "Rev. Stat.").
Session Laws
Finally, the table covers session laws, which are the laws as originally enacted in chronological order. The reporter is the United States Statutes at Large, abbreviated "Stat." The citation format is the volume number, then "Stat.," then the page number, with the year in parentheses. The table notes that public laws enacted before 1957 should be cited by their chapter number, while public laws enacted from 1957 onward should be cited by their public law number.]

### Department of the Treasury

[Department of the Treasury (pp. 265–67)
