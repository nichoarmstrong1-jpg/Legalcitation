# General Citation Rules — Bluebook B1-B9 + Rules 1-9

## Bluepages (Practitioner Rules)

### Introduction

The Bluepages provide a uniform system of citation for legal practitioners, with these core principles:
- Follow local court rules first (see jurisdiction-specific guidance in BT2).
- Bluepages use underscoring tradition; italics may be substituted if used consistently.
- Where Bluepages are silent, supplement with White Pages rules and apply Bluepages typeface differences.
- A citation generally includes: signal, authority, and parenthetical information.

### B1 Structure of Legal Citations

#### B1.1 Citation Sentences and Clauses
- Citation sentences begin with a capital letter and end with a period.
- Citation clauses are comma-set, not capitalized unless required, and typically do not end with a period unless last in sentence.
- Use citation sentences for authorities supporting the entire preceding sentence.
- Use citation clauses for authorities supporting only part of a sentence.

#### B1.2 Introductory Signals
- Signals communicate authority-proposition relationship.
- Capitalize signals in citation sentences; lowercase in citation clauses.
- Signal families include: `[no signal]`, `e.g.`, `accord`, `see`, `see also`, `cf.`, `compare ... with ...`, `contrast ... with ...`, `contra`, `but see`, `but cf.`, `see generally`.
- Use semicolons to separate authorities within each signal group.
- Order signals per Bluebook sequence.

#### B1.3 Explanatory Parentheticals
- Parentheticals may explain authority relevance and appear before subsequent history.
- Generally begin with a present participle unless quoting a full sentence.
- Do not capitalize or end with period unless parenthetical is a full-sentence quote.
- Follow White Pages rule 1.5 for fuller ordering/detail guidance.

### B2 Typeface for Court Documents

Court documents generally use ordinary type and italics (or underscoring equivalent). In citations, italicize/underscore:
- Introductory signals
- Case names (including procedural phrases)
- Titles of books and articles
- Some legislative titles
- Explanatory phrases introducing subsequent history
- Cross-references such as `id.` and `supra`
- Phrases introducing related authority such as "quoted in"

### B3 Subdivisions

- Give page numbers before date parentheticals (no `p.` or `at.` unless needed to avoid confusion).
- Cite nonconsecutive pages with commas.
- Use `n.` for footnotes, volume numbers in Arabic, and section/paragraph symbols where applicable.

### B4 Short Citation Forms

- Use `id.` only for the immediately preceding authority and only when that citation has a single authority.
- Use `id. at <pin>` for different page in same source.
- `supra` / `hereinafter` allowed for many non-case/non-statute source categories, but generally not for cases/statutes/constitutions/restatements/model codes/regulations except extraordinary circumstances.

### B5 Quotations

#### B5.1 Generally
- Quotations under 50 words use quotation marks.
- Commas/periods stay inside quotation marks; other punctuation outside unless part of quoted text.

#### B5.2 Block Quotations
- 50+ words: block quote, single-spaced, indented both sides, no quotation marks.
- Citation appears on next line at left margin.

#### B5.3 Modifying Quotations and Citations for Clarity
- Permitted readability cleanups may be indicated with `(citation modified)`.
- Additional alterations still require regular alteration/omission notation.

### B6 Abbreviations, Numerals, and Symbols

- Use Bluebook tables for abbreviations (T5–T16).
- Close up adjacent single capitals (`U.S.`), but not single caps with longer abbreviations (`S. Ct.`).
- Spell out zero through ninety-nine in text (with standard exceptions).
- Optional closer reporter abbreviation forms may be used in constrained court-document contexts.

### B7 Italicization for Style and Unique Circumstances

- Words/phrases may be italicized (or underscored) for emphasis.
- Non-English terms may be italicized unless incorporated in common English usage.
- `Id.` is always italicized/underscored.

### B8 Capitalization

- Capitalize "Court" when naming courts in full or referring to the U.S. Supreme Court; in court docs also capitalize when referring to the receiving court.
- Capitalize party designations only when referring to parties in the present matter.
- Capitalize specific filed document titles; do not capitalize generic document labels.
- Do not abbreviate titles of court documents in textual sentences.

### B9 Titles of Judges

- Use forms like `Justice Story`, `Chief Justice Jay`, `Holmes, J.`, `Black & White, JJ.`, `Judge Hand`, `Chief Judge Friendly`.
- Follow White Pages rule 9 and Table T11 for broader title conventions.

## White Pages (Full Academic Rules)

### Rule 1: Structure and Use of Citations

#### 1.1 Citation Sentences and Clauses in Law Reviews
- Law review citations generally appear in footnotes.
- Citation sentences support whole propositions; citation clauses support partial propositions.
- Call numbers placement follows punctuation conventions.

#### 1.2 Introductory Signals
- Defines supportive, comparative, contradictory, and background signals.
- `see also`, `cf.`, `compare ... with ...`, `contrast ... with ...`, `but cf.`, and `see generally` require explanatory parentheticals where prescribed.
- Pincites are required except with `see generally`.

#### 1.3 Order of Signals
- Order signal groups by Rule 1.2 sequence.
- Same-type signals may be strung in one citation sentence with semicolons.
- Different signal families are generally grouped separately (except as allowed in citation clauses).

#### 1.4 Order of Authorities Within Each Signal
- Separate authorities with semicolons.
- Order by helpfulness/authoritativeness in a logical sequence.
- Short-form authorities are ordered as if cited in full.

#### 1.5 Parenthetical Information
- Use explanatory parentheticals where relevance is not self-evident.
- Non-quote parentheticals generally start with present participles and lowercase.
- Quoted full-sentence parentheticals begin with capital and include closing punctuation.
- Parenthetical ordering and nesting follow Rule 1.5(b), including placement relative to URLs, archival links, explanatory parentheticals, and prior/subsequent history.

#### 1.6 Related Authority
- Use italicized explanatory phrases to append related authorities (e.g., `in`, `reprinted in`, `quoted in`, `cited with approval in`).
- Include relevant prior/subsequent history where required by Rules 10.7 and 12.7.

### Rule 2: Typography for Law Reviews

#### 2.1 Typeface Conventions for Citations
- Uses ordinary roman, italics, and small caps.
- Full case names in full citations are roman (except procedural phrases); short-form case names are italicized.
- Book authors/titles are small caps.
- Periodical article titles are italicized; periodical names are small caps.
- Signals and explanatory phrases are italicized in citation contexts.
- Punctuation is italicized only when part of italicized material.

#### 2.2 Typeface Conventions for Textual Material
- Main text generally roman with italics for case names, publication titles, and style emphasis.
- Footnote text follows mixed rules depending on whether reference is grammatical text or citation clause.
- Ellipses/brackets for alterations are romanized.

#### 2.3 Other Punctuation Conventions
- Use one space after punctuation in proportional fonts.
- Double spacing after punctuation is only for monospaced fonts.

### Rule 3: Subdivisions

#### 3.1 Volumes, Parts, and Supplements
- Identify separately paginated subdivisions.
- Volume numbers are Arabic numerals.
- Handle series/year-bind formats with bracket conventions where needed.

#### 3.2 Pages, Footnotes, Endnotes, and Graphical Materials
- Page references precede date parentheticals.
- Use pincites for specific internal references.
- Use `n.` / `nn.` for footnotes; follow ampersand/comma conventions for same-page notes.
- Cite tables/figures/charts using T16 abbreviations.

#### 3.3 Sections and Paragraphs
- Use `§` / `¶` in citations where appropriate.
- Use original punctuation where source separates section/subsection.
- Use `§§` / `¶¶` for multiple sections/paragraphs and apply range/scattered-section formatting conventions.

#### 3.4 Appended Material
- Cite appendices/comments/notes using T16 abbreviations and appended-material conventions.

#### 3.5 Internal Cross-References
- Use `supra` for prior material, `infra` for later material.
- Use consistent internal naming for parts/sections/notes/pages/figures/tables.

### Rule 4: Short Citation Forms

#### 4.1 `Id.`
- Permitted for immediately preceding authority under Bluebook conditions.
- Must indicate variation (e.g., different page or subdivision).
- Not permitted when preceding footnote contains multiple independent authorities.
- Sources only inside explanatory parentheticals/history are ignored for this limitation.

#### 4.2 `Supra` and `Hereinafter`
- Available for categories listed in Rule 4.2, generally not for cases/statutes/constitutions/restatements/model codes/regulations.
- `Supra` form usually includes author/short title + `supra` + note cross-reference + pinpoint.
- `Hereinafter` may establish custom short names when normal short forms are cumbersome/ambiguous.

### Rule 5: Quotations

#### 5.1 Formatting
- 50+ words: block-quote format.
- 49 or fewer words: inline quotation marks.
- Punctuation placement follows Bluebook quotation conventions.

#### 5.2 Alterations and Quotations Within Quotations
- Bracket case/word substitutions and insertions.
- Use `[sic]` for significant original mistakes.
- Indicate changes to quotation/citation via parentheticals (`alteration in original`, `emphasis added`, `citation omitted`, etc.).
- Quotation-within-quotation attribution follows quoting-parenthetical rules.

#### 5.3 Omissions
- Use ellipses for omissions within quoted matter following rule-specific patterns.
- Do not begin quotation with ellipsis.
- Omitted footnotes/citations are indicated parenthetically, not with ellipsis.

### Rule 6: Abbreviations, Numerals, and Symbols

#### 6.1 Abbreviations
- Use Bluebook abbreviation tables and avoid unlisted abbreviations unless unambiguous and materially space-saving.
- Spacing/period conventions govern abbreviations; certain spoken-initial entities may omit periods in limited contexts.

#### 6.2 Numerals and Symbols
- Spell out zero through ninety-nine in text unless exception applies.
- Use numeric formatting and ordinal conventions per rule.
- Use section/paragraph symbols in citations and word forms in text except specified exceptions.
- Use `$` and `%` symbols with numerals; symbols do not begin sentences.

### Rule 7: Italicization for Style and Unique Circumstances

- Italicize for emphasis where appropriate.
- Italicize foreign words not assimilated into English usage.
- `id.` is always italicized.
- Italicize hypothetical variable letters and certain typographic disambiguations (e.g., lowercase `l` subdivision markers).

### Rule 8: Capitalization

- Capitalize headings/titles under Bluebook title-case conventions.
- Preserve source capitalization for URLs and internet main page titles.
- Follow author-name capitalization as source provides.
- Apply legal-writing-specific capitalization rules for terms including `Act`, `Circuit`, `Code`, `Commonwealth`, `Constitution`, `Court`, `Federal`, `Judge/Justice`, `State`, and `Term` per context.

### Rule 9: Titles of Judges, Officials, and Terms of Court

- Use prescribed naming/parenthetical abbreviations for judges and justices.
- Order judge lists according to reporter/court conventions.
- Refer to court terms as `this term`, `last term`, or by year, with Supreme Court Term year keyed to start year.
