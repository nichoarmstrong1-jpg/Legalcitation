### Rule 14.2 Rules, Regulations, and Other Publications
[Here's a plain-text explanation of the diagram on the page:

It takes the example citation 7 C.F.R. § 319.76 (2019). and uses labeled brackets and arrows to show what each piece of the citation means.
Here's what each part represents, from left to right:

"7" — This is the C.F.R. title number. The Code of Federal Regulations is organized into numbered titles by subject area, and "7" tells you which title to look in.
"C.F.R." — This is the abbreviation of the set of regulations cited, standing for "Code of Federal Regulations." It tells the reader which regulatory publication you're referencing.
"§" — This is the section symbol, which introduces the specific section number that follows.
"319.76" — This is the specific section cited within that title. Together with the section symbol, this pinpoints the exact regulation.
"(2019)" — This is the date of the code edition cited. Since the C.F.R. is updated regularly, the year tells the reader which edition of the code you're referencing.
"." — A period ends the citation.
Why it matters: The diagram is essentially a teaching tool. It's showing legal writers and law students exactly how to construct a proper Bluebook citation for a federal regulation. Each component of the citation has a specific purpose, and the diagram makes it easy to see at a glance which part is which — so anyone formatting a legal citation can get it right. This is from Rule 14.2 of The Bluebook, which covers citing rules, regulations, and other administrative publications.
(a) Final rules and regulations.
 Whenever possible, cite federal rules and regulations to the Code of Federal Regulations (C.F.R.) by title, section or part, and year:
47 C.F.R. § 73.609 (2024).


14 C.F.R. § 43 app. B(a)(3) (2024).


Each title of the Code of Federal Regulations is revised at least once a year; cite the most recent edition. Give the name of the rule or regulation only if the rule or regulation is commonly cited that way or the information would otherwise aid in identification. The abbreviated name of the issuing body may also be included if helpful:


FCC Broadcast Radio Services, 47 C.F.R. § 73.609 (2024).


FTC Credit Practices Rule, 16 C.F.R. § 444.1 (2024).


Credit Practices Rule, 16 C.F.R. § 444.1 (2024).


Certain titles of the Code of Federal Regulations focused on specific subjects have unique citations. For citation to Title 26, Treasury Regulations, see table T1.2. Title 48, the Federal Acquisition Regulations, may be cited as “FAR”:



FAR 52.249-2(e) (2019).


The Federal Register (Fed. Reg.) publishes rules and regulations before they are entered into the Code of Federal Regulations. Citations to rules or regulations in the Federal Register should give any commonly used name of the rule or regulation, the volume and page on which the rule or regulation (or any preceding discussion thereof) begins, and the date of the rule or regulation. When citing a part of a rule or regulation, give both the page on which the rule or regulation (or preceding discussion) begins and the page(s) on which the cited material appears. When the Federal Register indicates where the rule or regulation will appear in the Code of Federal Regulations, give that information parenthetically:



Importation of Fruits and Vegetables, 60 Fed. Reg. 50379, 50381 (Sep. 29, 1995) (to be codified at 7 C.F.R. pts. 300, 319).


Federal Acquisition Regulations for National Aeronautics and Space Administration, 55 Fed. Reg. 52782 (Dec. 21, 1990) (to be codified at 48 C.F.R. pt. 1).


Prohibitively long titles may be shortened as long as the result is unambiguous:


Order Approving NYSE and NASDAQ Proposed Rule Changes Relating to Equity Compensation Plans


Not: Self-Regulatory Organizations; New York Stock Exchange, Inc. and National Association of Securities Dealers, Inc.; Order Approving NYSE and NASDAQ Proposed Rule Changes and NASDAQ Amendment No. 1 and Notice of Filing and Order Granting Accelerated Approval to NYSE Amendments No. 1 and 2 and NASDAQ Amendments No. 2 and 3 Thereto Relating to Equity Compensation Plans


(b) Proposed rules, other notices, comments, and petitions for rulemaking.
 Administrative notices that are not transferred to the Code of Federal Regulations should be cited to the Federal Register. When citing notices of proposed rules and regulations, follow the form for final rules, but add the status to the date parenthetical:
Control of Air Pollution from New Motor Vehicles and New Motor Vehicle Engines, 56 Fed. Reg. 9754 (proposed Mar. 7, 1991) (to be codified at 40 C.F.R. pt. 86).


Cite notices pertaining to administrative adjudications according to rule 14.3. Cite other administrative notices by volume, page, and date. The citation may begin with a description or a commonly used name:



Meeting Notice, 65 Fed. Reg. 3415 (Jan. 21, 2000).


For rules and announcements not appearing in the Code of Federal Regulations or the Federal Register, cite a service (rule 19), the original form of issuance, or an agency or governmental website (rule 18.2). When citing comments, provide the name of the commenter and the proposed rule to which the comment pertains. Prohibitively long titles may be shortened as long as the result is unambiguous. Citing to an agency or governmental website is appropriate:



Chamber of Com. of the U.S., Comment Letter on Proposed Rule to Require Registration of Certain Hedge Fund Advisors Under the Investment Advisors Act of 1940 (Sep. 15, 2004), http://www.sec.gov/rules/proposed/s73004/dhirschmann091504.pdf.


When citing a petition for rulemaking, provide the title of the petition, the relevant page numbers, a parenthetical indicating the administrative agency the petition was filed with abbreviated according to table T6 and the date, and a link to the petition following rule 18.2:



Petition for Rulemaking and Request for Emergency Stay of Operation of Dedicated Short-Range Communications Service in the 5.850–5.9925 GHz Band (5.9 GHz Band) 8 (Fed. Commc’ns Comm’n June 28, 2016), https://publicknowledge.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/DSRC_PK_Petition_FINAL.pdf [https://perma.cc/ZXL5-AXSA].


(c) Regular reports.
 Cite in the same manner as periodical materials (rule 16). Always give the abbreviated agency name first and then use the abbreviations for periodical names given in table T6 and T13:
4 NLRB Ann. Rep. 93 (1939).


1942 Att’y Gen. Ann. Rep. 22.


(d) Other publications.
 Cite as a work by an institutional author (rule 15.1(c)), including a serial number, if any (rule 15.7), unless issued as a congressional document (rule 13.4):
Off. of Mgmt. & Budget, Exec. Off. of the President, OMB Bull. No. 99-04, Revised Statistical Definitions of Metropolitan Areas (MAs) and Guidance on Uses of MA Definitions (1999).


U.S. Gov't Accountability Off., GAO-01-1163T, Commercial Aviation: A Framework for Considering Federal Financial Assistance (2001).


U.S. Equal Emp. Opportunity Comm’n, EEOCM1A, A Technical Assistance Manual on the Employment Provisions (Title I) of the Americans with Disabilities Act (1992).


U.S. Food & Drug Admin., Premarket Assessment of Pediatric Medical Devices: Guidance for Industry and Food and Drug Administration Staff (2014).


When citing an opinion letter, cite as a work by an institutional author in ordinary roman type, including the date of its publication and, if available, its subject matter:


U.S. Equal Emp. Opportunity Comm'n, Opinion Letter on Applicability of Age Discrimination in Employment Act to Appointed State Court Judges (Apr. 7, 1987), as reprinted in EEOC Compliance Manual (BL) at N:1001 n.2.


U.S. Dep’t of Lab., Wage & Hour Div., Opinion Letter (Sep. 14, 1992). ]


### Rule 14.3 Administrative Adjudications and Arbitrations
[14.3 Administrative Adjudications and Arbitrations (pp. 150–52)
Citations to administrative cases, arbitrations, and filings or other documents in those cases and arbitrations should conform to rule 10, except as follows:


14.3.1 – Names
(a) Administrative adjudications.
 Cite by the reported name of the first-listed private party (abbreviated according to rule 10.2) or by the official subject-matter title. Omit all procedural phrases:
Trojan Transp., Inc., 249 NLRB 642 (1980).


Not: In the Matter of Trojan Transp., Inc., 249 NLRB 642 (1980).


Not: In re Trojan Transp., Inc., 249 NLRB 642 (1980).


For agencies that have multiple layers of internal adjudication, include a parenthetical indicating the adjudicatory body (abbreviated according to tables T6 and T11):



Tyson Farms, Inc., 72 Agric. Dec. 399 (U.S.D.A. A.L.J. 2013).


Steve Lane, 77 Agric. Dec. 246 (U.S.D.A. Jud. Officer 2018).


Subject-matter titles may sometimes indicate the nature and stage of an adjudicatory proceeding. It is permissible to shorten such titles, and, if the nature and stage of the proceeding are not clear from the context, such information may be included in a parenthetical phrase at the end of the citation. The parenthetical phrase may consist of terms such as “notice,” “initiation,” “prelim. neg.,” or “determination,” or may be more elaborate:



Bottled Green Olives from Spain, 50 Fed. Reg. 28237 (Dep’t of Com. July 11, 1985) (final admin. rev.).


Not: Bottled Green Olives from Spain, Final Results of Admin. Rev. of Countervailing Duty Order, 50 Fed. Reg. 28237 (Dep’t of Com. July 11, 1985).


(b) Arbitrations.
 Cite as court cases if adversary parties are named and as administrative adjudications if they are not. The arbitrator’s name should be indicated parenthetically:
Kroger Co. v. Amalgamated Meat Cutters, Local 539, 74 Lab. Arb. Rep. (BL) 785, 787 (1980) (Doering, Arb.).


Charles P. Ortmeyer, 23 Indus. Arb. 272 (1980) (Stern, Arb.).

14.3.2 – Which Source(s) to Cite
(a) Official reporters.
 Cite the official reporter of the agency if the opinion appears therein:
Tenn. Intrastate Rates & Charges, 286 I.C.C. 41 (1952).


For the official reporters of many federal agencies, see table T1.2.



(b) Official releases and slip opinions.
 If the opinion does not appear in an official reporter, cite the official release or slip opinion. Provide the full date, any helpful publication number, and the number of the case or investigation:
Iron Constr. Castings from Braz., Can., India & China, Inv. No. 701-TA-249, USITC Pub. 1720 (June 1985) (Preliminary).


If the opinion will later be published in an official bound volume, provide the volume number and the initial page number, if available; if the initial page number is not available, retain the case number:



Rosenberg Libr. Ass’n, 269 NLRB No. 197 (Apr. 24, 1984).


Whenever possible, append a parallel citation to an unofficial reporter, service, or other source, in that order of preference:



Rosenberg Libr. Ass’n, 269 NLRB No. 197, 1983–1984 NLRB Dec. (CCH) ¶ 16,238 (Apr. 24, 1984).


Once the official reporter is issued, however, cite only to that reporter:



Rosenberg Libr. Ass’n, 269 NLRB 1173 (1984).


(c) Services and electronic databases.
 Where an agency decision is only available from a service or on the agency's website, cite according to rule 19 (for services) or rule 18.2 (for internet sources):
Carr, [1990–1992 Transfer Binder] Comm. Fut. L. Rep. (CCH) ¶ 24,933 (Oct. 2, 1990).


MatchNet PLC v. Gordon, Case No. AF-001060 (eResolution Dec. 4, 2001), http://www.disputes.org/decisions/1060.htm (on file with the Yale Law Journal).


Many agencies have adopted unique citation formats for opinions not listed in official reporters. Consult table T1.2 for agency-specific citation formats. For a list of services and service abbreviations, see table T15.


14.3.3 – Issuing Agency
If the name of the issuing agency is not apparent from the name of the source, include the name of the agency abbreviated according to rule 15.1(e) in the parenthetical containing the date:



Gen. Dynamics Corp., 50 Fed. Reg. 45949 (U.S. Dep’t of Lab. Nov. 5, 1985). ]

### Rule 14.6 Short Forms for Regulations
[Here's a plain-text explanation of the chart on this page:

**What it is:** This page is Bluebook Rule 14.6 — "Short Forms for Regulations." It contains a **reference table** that shows how to shorten legal citations for regulations depending on where and how you're using them. The table has **4 columns** and **2 example rows**, one for each major type of regulatory source.

---

**The 4 columns explained:**

1. **(No header / Source type)** — The first column identifies which type of regulatory publication is being cited. The two types shown are "Code of Federal Regulations" and "Federal Register."

2. **"Full citation"** — This column shows the complete, formal citation you'd use the first time you reference a regulation (or when there's no nearby prior citation). It includes every detail: the name of the rule, the title number, the section, and the date/year.

3. **"Text"** — This column shows what you write when you mention a regulation in the body of your text (either main text or footnote text). It's a shortened, plain-language reference — typically just the name of the rule or regulation — without the full legal citation apparatus.

4. **"Short citation"** — This column shows the abbreviated citation forms you can use after you've already given the full citation nearby (within the same footnote or in one of the five preceding footnotes). It's a condensed version that still pinpoints the regulation but drops unnecessary details.

---

**The 2 rows (examples) explained:**

**Row 1 — Code of Federal Regulations (C.F.R.):**
- Full citation: "FTC Credit Practices Rule, 16 C.F.R. § 444.1 (2014)" — the complete cite with rule name, title, section, and year.
- Text form: "16 C.F.R. § 444.1" — just the title, abbreviation, and section number, no year or rule name.
- Short citation: "16 C.F.R. § 444.1" or just "§ 444.1" — you can drop even more if the context is clear.

**Row 2 — Federal Register:**
- Full citation: "Importation of Fruits and Vegetables, 60 Fed. Reg. 50379, 50381 (Sep. 29, 1995) (to be codified at 7 C.F.R. pt. 300)" — the complete cite with name, volume, page numbers, date, and where it will eventually appear in the C.F.R.
- Text form: "Importation of Fruits and Vegetables" — just the name of the rule/notice.
- Short citation: "Importation of Fruits and Vegetables, 60 Fed. Reg. at 50381" — the name plus a pinpoint page reference using "at."

---

**The surrounding text (sections a–d) explains *when* to use each column:**

- **(a) Main text** — When you mention a regulation in the body of a law review article, use the "Text" column form and put a full or short citation in a footnote.
- **(b) Footnote text** — Same idea: when referring to a regulation inside footnote prose, use the "Text" column form and add a citation clause.
- **(c) Citations** — When writing an actual citation (in a footnote's citation sentence or clause), use the "Short citation" form if the regulation has already been fully cited nearby. If not, use the "Full citation" form.
- **(d) Electronic sources** — For electronic versions, see Rule 18.12.

**In short,** the table is a quick-reference guide that tells legal writers: "Here's what the full version looks like, here's what to write in your prose, and here's how much you can abbreviate when citing it again." ]
