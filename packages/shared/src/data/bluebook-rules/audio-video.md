# Audio, Video & Streaming — Bluebook B18 + Rules 18.7-18.8

## Bluepages
### B18 The Internet
#### B18.1 Full Citation
[B18.1 Full Citation (p.28)
B18.1.1 – Direct Citations
A full citation of an Internet source includes: (1) the name of the author(s) (if applicable); (2) the title of the specific page of the website, such as a posting or comment (underlined or italicized) (if applicable); (3) the title of the main page of the website abbreviated according to tables T6 and T10; (4) the date and time (if applicable); and (5) the URL. For further guidance on citing Internet sources, see rule 18.2:



David Lat, Movie Night with Justice Breyer, Above the L. (Oct. 27, 2014, at 15:31 ET), https://abovethelaw.com/2014/10/movie-night-with-justice-breyer/.


Fabrizio di Piazza, SCOTUSblog on Camera: Laurence H. Tribe (Part One), SCOTUSblog, https://www.scotusblog.com/media/scotusblog-on-camera-laurence-h-tribe-part-one/.


Eugene Kontorovich, Italy Adopts Supreme Court's View of ICJ Authority, Wash. Post (Oct. 28, 2014, at 10:41 ET), https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/volokh-conspiracy/wp/2014/10/28/italy-adopts-supreme-courts-view-of-icj-authority/.


Bluepages Tip:
If a source is available in both HTML and PDF (which preserves the pagination of the print version), then you should cite to only the PDF. Whenever possible, provide a pincite for internet sources:



Ulrich Cubasch et al., Climate Change 2013: The Physical Science Basis, Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, 121 (2013), https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar5/wg1/.


If there is no date associated with the specific webpage, use the date on which you “last visited” the webpage:



Yahoo! Home Page, http://www.yahoo.com (last visited Mar. 18, 2020).

B18.1.2 – Parallel Citations
For printed material, you may provide a parallel citation to an internet source:



Am. Mining Cong. v. U.S. Army Corps of Eng’rs, No. CIV. A. 93-1754 SSH (D.D.C. Jan. 23, 1997), http://www.wetlands.com/fed/tulloch1.htm.]

#### B18.2 Short Form Citation
[B18.2 Short Form Citation (p.28)
After providing a full citation to an internet source, you may use “id.” or “supra” in subsequent citations per rules 4 and 18.12:



Lat, supra.]



## White Pages (Full Academic Rules)
### Rule 18.7 Videographic Media
[18.7 Videographic Media (pp. 193–95)
18.7.1 – Films
When citing commercial films, documentaries, or other full-length movies, cite the name of the film in small capitals, the access medium, and timestamp (if applicable). Include the names of the individuals, company, or network that produced the film and the year of release in a parenthetical. If no access medium is available, the film should be made available on file and a parenthetical should be appended describing where it can be found.



When citing a specific quote or scene, use time markers to designate pinpoint citations, using the phrase “, at XX:XX” or “, at XX:XX–XX:XX” immediately after the title.



(a) Commercial.
 A commercial film is any film made for public release and made commercially available through distribution in theaters, or through others medium such as recordings or streaming:
Field of Dreams, Blu-ray (Gordon Company 1989).


Another Body, Amazon Prime, at 1:00:25 (Utopia 2023).


(b) Noncommercial.
 A noncommercial film is any film made for non-commercial or artistic purposes.
For noncommercial films, cite the person or institution that produced the film (if available), the name of the film in small capitals, the access medium (if available), the timestamp (if applicable), and the year of release:
Alex Bateman, Walking on Tea Leaves (2024) (on file with the Columbia Law Review).


Luis Buñuel, Un Chien Andalou, DVD (1929).

18.7.2 – Television Series
Cite a television series by the name of the series in small capitals with the relevant episode name (if applicable) in italics, followed by the access medium in a parenthetical. Include in the medium parenthetical the date of the broadcast or the date of release (or, if unknown, the date of access):



The Queen’s Gambit (Netflix, accessed Nov. 7, 2024).


Community: Remedial Chaos Theory (NBC television broadcast, aired Oct. 13, 2011).


Bill Nye the Science Guy: The Planets (DVD, released 2001).

18.7.3 – Live Streaming Media Services
When possible, citation to a live stream or live broadcast should be made to the documented video of that live stream per rule 18.7.4. If no public recording of the live stream exists, the following format should be used and a recording should be stored on file. In this instance, a parenthetical must be appended to indicate where the recording can be found.



(a) Commercial.
 Commercial live streams are any videos live streamed on a platform that requires payment for access to the platform or to the live stream itself. Cite a commercial live stream by the name of the broadcast and the timestamp (if applicable). Indicate parenthetically the platform on which the video was streamed and the original date of the stream. The video should be stored on file and a parenthetical should be appended describing where it can be found:
NCAAW Iowa vs. South Carolina, at 1:49:50 (Fubo, aired Apr. 7, 2024, at 16:49 ET) (on file with the Yale Law Journal).


(b) Noncommercial.
 Noncommercial live streams are any videos live streamed on a platform that is free to access for end users. Cite a noncommercial live stream by the name of the account that streamed the video in small capitals and the name of the stream in italics. Parenthetically provide the name of the streaming platform and include the date and start time of the stream (when possible) in the platform parenthetical. The video should be stored on file and a parenthetical should be appended describing where it can be found:
DJAmber_D, Studio Session (Twitch, streamed Apr. 12, 2024, at 15:05 ET) (on file with the Columbia Law Review).

18.7.4 – Web-Based Videos
Web-based videos are videographic media originally published online. This does not include short-form social media content in the form of video, which should be cited in accordance with rule 18.10.



For web-based videos, cite by the name of the account that posted the video using small capitals, the title of the video in italics, and the timestamp (if applicable). Indicate the platform where the video was uploaded and the date and time of the upload (when possible) in a parenthetical. The platform indicated should be the platform on which the video was first uploaded by the creator.



Timestamps should be designated by pinpoint citations, using the phrase “, at XX:XX” or “, at XX:XX–XX:XX” immediately after the title of the video:



hbomberguy, Plagiarism and You(Tube), at 34:19 (YouTube, Dec. 2, 2023), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yDp3cB5fHXQ&t=8233s (on file with the University of Pennsylvania Law Review).


International Bar Association, Black Lives Matter: A Challenge to the Law (Vimeo, June 17, 2020, at 04:49 ET), https://vimeo.com/429916976 (on file with the Harvard Law Review).]

### Rule 18.8 Audio Recordings and Streaming
[18.8 Audio Recordings and Streaming (pp. 195–97)
18.8.1 – Physical Media
(a) Commercial recordings.
 A commercial recording is any audio recording made commercially available through distribution in physical media. Cite commercial recordings by creator in small capitals, title of the work, access medium (if available), and by providing the name of the recording company or publisher (abbreviated per rule 15.1(e)) and the date of release (if available):
Mitski, Be the Cowboy (CD, Dead Oceans Aug. 17, 2018).


The Killers, Sam’s Town (Vinyl Record, Island Recs. Sep. 27, 2006).


If a commercial recording was released independently, the phrase “independently recorded” should be used in lieu of the recording company:



Car Seat Headrest, Twin Fantasy (Cassette, independently recorded Nov. 2, 2011).


If a particular song, musical work, or segment of an audio work is referred to, cite it by analogy to shorter works in a collection according to rule 15.5.1. Include the access medium (if available) after the title of the collection and before any time marker. Time markers may be used for designating pinpoint citations, using the phrase “, at XX:XX” immediately after the title of the collection, but they are not required:



Gang of Youths, Achilles Come Down, on Go Farther In Lightness (CD, Sony Aug. 18, 2017).


Young the Giant, Mind over Matter, on Mind over Matter, at 00:30–00:32 (CD, Fueled by Ramen Jan. 21, 2014).


If the particular song, musical work, or segment of an audio work being referred to was released as a single or standalone product, cite it as you would cite a segment of a larger audio work with a bracket indicating that the work is a single.



Electric Light Orchestra, Can't Get It out Of My Head, on Can’t Get It out of My Head [Single] (Vinyl Record, Warner Bros. Recs. Jan. 1975).

(b) Noncommercial recordings.
A noncommercial recording is any audio recording made for non-commercial or art purposes, and not commercially available to the public.

For non-commercial recordings, cite the person or institution that produced the recording (if available) in small capitals, the name of the recording, and a parenthetical indicating the access medium, and the date on which the recording was created. The recording should be stored on file and a parenthetical should be appended describing where it can be found. Time markers may be used for designating pinpoint citations, using the phrase “, at XX:XX” immediately after the name of the recording, but they are not required:
Franklin Middle School Jazz Band, Winter Holiday All-School Concert, at 00:12:35–00:15:54 (CD, Dec. 18, 2010) (on file with author).

(c) Episodic recordings.
For audio recordings produced and distributed in an episodic format, include the title of the recording in small capitals with the relevant episode name (if applicable) in italics, followed by the access medium in a parenthetical. Include in the access medium parenthetical the date of release. Time markers may be used for designating pinpoint citations, using the phrase “, at XX:XX” immediately after the name of the episode, but they are not required:
The Twilight Zone Radio Dramas: A Kind of Stopwatch (CD, Aug. 26, 2002).

18.8.2 – Audio Streaming Services
A recording may be cited to an audio streaming service only when it has not been published in a physical medium, or if the version available on audio streaming services contains a difference in content relevant to the citation.

Cite a recording found on an audio streaming service as a physical commercial recording per rule 18.8.1(a), except by replacing the name of the media storage device with the name of the audio streaming service. If the release date of the recording is unavailable, indicate the date when the work was last streamed. Time markers may be used for designating pinpoint citations, using the phrase “, at XX:XX” immediately after the title of the collection, but they are not required:



Max Cornell, Hen (Bandcamp, independently recorded Mar. 27, 2020).


Foreign Ghost, How Long Have I Been Living In the Shadow Realm, on Foreign Ghost (Apple Music, independently recorded July 31, 2019).


Childish Gambino, This Is America, on This Is America [Single] (Apple Music, RCA Recs. May 6, 2018).


For audio recordings produced and distributed in an episodic format on an audio streaming service, such as a podcast, cite the work according to rule 18.8.1(c), except by replacing the name of the access medium with the name of the streaming service. If the date of release is unavailable, indicate the date when the work was last streamed. Time markers may be used for designating pinpoint citations, using the phrase “, at XX:XX” immediately after the name of the episode, but they are not required:



Strict Scrutiny: The Legality of Presidents Doing Whatever They Want (Spotify, Jan. 15, 2024).

18.8.3 – Unpublished Audio Recordings
When citing an unpublished audio recording, including recorded phone calls, identify the nature of the recording, and (when possible) indicate the recorder or producer by name, title, and/or institutional affiliation. Abbreviate title and institutional affiliation according to tables T6 and T13. Time markers may be used for designating pinpoint citations, using the phrase “, at XX:XX” immediately after the nature of the recording, but they are not required:



Voice Memo, at 00:54, Jacob A. Sarasin, Legal Observer, Nat’l Laws. Guild (recorded Apr. 17, 2024, at 08:02 ET) (on file with author).


Voice Message from Nikhil Mahadeva, Bluebook Ed., Columbia L. Rev., to Luke Cronin, Bluebook Ed., Columbia L. Rev. (recorded June 16, 2024, at 19:15 ET) (on file with the Columbia Law Review).

18.8.4 – Websites Containing Audio Recordings
Audio recordings embedded in a webpage should be cited using the principles of rule 18.2.2. Time markers may be used for designating pinpoint citations, using the phrase “, at XX:XX” immediately after the main page title, but they are not required:



The New Yorker Radio Hour, Mass Incarceration, Then and Now, New Yorker, at 01:03 (Jan. 17, 2020), https://www.newyorker.com/podcast/the-new-yorker-radio-hour/mass-incarceration-then-and-now (on file with the Harvard Law Review).]
